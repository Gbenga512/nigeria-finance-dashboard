# MScFE Capstone Research Question

## Working Project Title

**Development and Evaluation of a Quantitative Financial Intelligence System for Market Risk, Portfolio Risk and Decision Support in Emerging Markets**

## Research Problem

Financial decision-makers face uncertainty from market volatility, changing correlations, extreme losses and shifts in market regimes. Conventional dashboards can display financial information but may provide limited quantitative evidence about tail risk, portfolio risk, model reliability and the stability of decisions across changing market conditions.

This project addresses that problem by developing an integrated quantitative financial intelligence system that combines market-risk measurement, portfolio risk analysis, volatility-regime analysis, machine learning, walk-forward evaluation, statistical validation, stress testing and robustness analysis.

## Central Research Question

**Can an integrated quantitative financial intelligence system improve the measurement, validation and interpretation of market and portfolio risk across emerging and global financial markets?**

## Specific Research Questions

1. How do historical, parametric and Monte Carlo approaches differ in their estimates of Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES)?
2. How sensitive are risk estimates to confidence levels and estimation-window choices?
3. Does dynamic minimum-variance portfolio allocation provide improved risk-adjusted performance relative to an equal-weight benchmark in out-of-sample testing?
4. Can observable market characteristics such as momentum, trend and volatility provide useful information about future volatility regimes?
5. Does machine-learning-based volatility-regime prediction provide useful out-of-sample information relative to a persistence benchmark?
6. How robust are the principal findings under alternative models, estimation windows and adverse stress scenarios?

## General Objective

To develop and empirically evaluate an integrated quantitative financial intelligence system for market-risk measurement, portfolio-risk analysis and financial decision support.

## Specific Objectives

1. To estimate market risk using historical, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR and Expected Shortfall methods.
2. To compare the behaviour and model risk of alternative market-risk methodologies.
3. To evaluate portfolio risk and dynamic minimum-variance allocation against an equal-weight benchmark.
4. To identify volatility regimes using observable market characteristics.
5. To evaluate machine-learning models for out-of-sample volatility-regime prediction.
6. To assess strategy and portfolio performance using chronological walk-forward validation and realistic transaction costs.
7. To statistically validate risk-model forecasts and examine model exceptions and dependence.
8. To assess the robustness of findings through sensitivity analysis, bootstrap uncertainty and stress testing.
9. To implement the quantitative framework as an operational decision-support layer within NG Finance Pro.

## Research Contribution

The intended contribution is an integrated and reproducible quantitative framework that connects risk measurement, portfolio allocation, regime analysis, machine learning and model validation within a single financial intelligence environment. The contribution will be evaluated empirically rather than assumed; final claims will be based on out-of-sample results, statistical tests and robustness evidence.

## Scope

The empirical study will use selected Nigerian-linked and global financial instruments, including USD/NGN and selected cross-asset instruments where reliable historical observations are available. The final asset universe and observation period will be frozen before the principal experiments and documented in the methodology.

## Research Integrity Rule

No final performance or superiority claim will be made until the corresponding experiment has been completed on the predefined evaluation data. Model selection must not use the final test set. All material results reported in the capstone must be reproducible from the project source code and documented experiment configuration.
